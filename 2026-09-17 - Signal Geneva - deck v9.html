03 · Section 1 · The spectrum
Slide 3 · How they communicate · Three models of human and agent communication

When agents start talking

As everyone gets their own agents, the real question becomes who talks to whom. Three models, each more powerful, and more tangled, than the last. Start from personal agents and progress only as your trust, guardrails, risk, security and ROI mature.

Start here ↓Maturity →
01 · today

You and your agents

You talk to your own agents. One human directing their own fleet. AI never sends messages on your behalf.

02 · plus ones

Every function gets a +1

Finance, HR and IT each have a virtual +1 that anyone in the company can talk to, through the same chat and collaboration tools people already use. (The "Plus One" model.)

03 · agents ↔ agents

Agents talk to agents

Your agents talk to other people's agents, then report back to humans. Hugely powerful, and complex very fast.

Your collaboration tools become the substrate for agent collaboration. The new governance question isn't "can we use AI?", it's who is allowed to talk to whom and about what. Mature your use of agents "as fast as (safely) possible, but no faster."

90 sec Once everyone has agents, the question is who talks to whom. Three cards, left to right, once.

  • 01: you direct your own agents. Nothing is sent on your behalf.
  • 02: every function gets a +1 that anyone can talk to, in the chat tools people already use. The model, not a case study.
  • 03: agents talk to other people's agents and report back. Hugely powerful, complex very fast.
  • Start at one. Move right as trust, guardrails, security and ROI mature: "as fast as (safely) possible, but no faster."
  • Governance: not "can we use AI?" but who is allowed to talk to whom, and about what. An organisational question, not a capability one.
  • Bridge: "So that is the spectrum, twice, and the shape of the conversation. Now the uncomfortable part. Almost everyone is at model one, at step one, and it has not moved in a year."

Slide 7 · The diagnosis

So why is it stuck?

The capability shipped. But adoption and impact are lagging. Five things hold the second spectrum still, and every one of them is organisational.

Governance and the org itself

Managers of managers of managers, and the meeting calendar that holds them together.

AI put under corporate IT

The eighteen-month enterprise chatbot project: the data warehouse and the data lake wearing a new coat.

Trust

The CIO's view: risk, data access, hosting, model choice, sovereignty. Every one of them a legitimate delay.

The non-functional tax

Infrastructure, cost, routing, hosting, evaluation. Every point solution solving all of it again, for a handful of users.

Pace

The industry moves faster than a procurement cycle. No pick-it-up-and-go culture.

32%
expected AI-driven headcount cuts, asked in 2025
14%
actually saw them a year later
"The limiting factor is increasingly the organization's ability to absorb change." Tara Balakrishnan · associate partner, McKinsey · p24 of the report
McKinsey & Company / QuantumBlack, The state of AI in 2026: On the road to ROI, August 2026: Balakrishnan quote p24; 14% and 32% p25 and Exhibit 16. Five causes and anti-patterns: Sam Bourton.

2 min The capability shipped, adoption and impact lag. Five causes, every one organisational. Land one and two, gesture at the rest.

  • 01 Governance and the org itself. Managers of managers of managers, and the meeting calendar that holds them together. The comfort zone is a process, and processes are what agents cross.
  • 02 AI put under corporate IT. The eighteen-month enterprise chatbot: the data warehouse and the data lake wearing a new coat. By the time it lands you would build it differently.
  • 03 Trust. The CIO view, and the risks are real: data access, hosting, model choice, sovereignty. Each a legitimate delay.
  • 04 The non-functional tax. Infrastructure, cost, routing, model selection, hosting, evaluation, A/B testing. Every point solution solves all of it again, for a handful of users, because there is no common platform.
  • 05 Pace. The industry moves faster than a procurement cycle. Anything specified twelve months ago is now the wrong design, and everybody knows it while they finish building it.
  • Anti-patterns: AI reporting into IT. The 18-month chatbot. Specified last year, shipped next year. Governance as the whole programme. One agent, one person, declared done.
  • 14 versus 32: 32% expected AI-driven headcount cuts in 2025, 14% saw them. The constraint is organisational absorption, not capability.
  • If asked: the itemised list of non-functional requirements (48 platform capabilities) is Sohrab Hosseini's, at Orq.ai. No named source for "there is no common platform", so it stays unattributed.

90 sec What a change has to touch. An anatomy of cost, not the scope ladder from slide 2.

  • Read from the middle outwards, once: "Code. Architecture. Teams. The organisation. Every AI tool you have been sold this year lives in the inner two. Every number you are waiting for lives in the outer two." Then stop.
  • Horizontal tools touch the inner rings. Engineering teams adopt them fast and nobody outside has to agree. That is why adoption looks universal.
  • Vertical products cross departments and workflows, so they touch every ring. That is why the returns look like nothing, and why the few that work are so hard to copy.
  • Investor turn: "What you are underwriting is ring depth. A product that stops at the two inner rings has a fast sales cycle, a small contract and no defence. One that reaches the outer two has a slow sales cycle, a large contract, and a moat made of somebody else's process. Right now the market prices them the same."
  • Diligence question: how far out does the product reach before the customer gets value, and did anyone there have to change behaviour? If nobody did, there is nothing to defend.
  • Keep the individual, team, workflow, function, company words on slide 2.

Slide 12 · What to fund

Three signals I look for

The moat is moving to process knowledge and the org interface. The model and the agent are becoming inputs. But we don't have the maturity to assess agent quality yet, only that we can tell when it doesn't work.

01 · Real experience inside the client

Someone who has sat in the organisation being sold to. A senior operator paired with great technology, rather than two very smart people building agents for an industry neither has worked in.

02 · Workflow and organisation-level problems (and solutions)

Workflow and organisation-level problems rather than individual productivity: the second spectrum, step three and above. If the customer did not have to change how the work runs, there is nothing to defend.

03 · The NFRs already solved

Infrastructure, cost, routing, model selection, evaluation, hosting, done before the first customer. The shrink-wrapped feel of a consumer product, pointed at supply chain or procurement. Very few teams have it, and it shows in how few reach the workflow.

"We don't need to own the models, but we own the context. We own the interface which sits between your workflow and the models." Jeremy Palmer · PhysicsX · September 2026
"37-40% of AI time savings get eaten by review." Ryan Daniels and John Sarihan, Crosby · Sequoia Training Data · 2 September 2025
AI that comes with Trust baked in. The most important product metric is Time to Review.
Jeremy Palmer, PhysicsX, September 2026. Crosby quote: Ryan Daniels and John Sarihan on Sequoia Capital's Training Data podcast, 2 September 2025.
